Our purpose

About the Office of First- and Second-Year Experiences

OFSYE works across CSUDH to create a more connected and supportive experience as students transition to the university, move through their first year, and build momentum during their second year.

Our mission

The Office of First- and Second-Year Experiences leads and supports campus efforts to help students transition successfully to CSUDH and progress through their second year.

OFSYE promotes integrated academic and co-curricular experiences that value and build upon the cultures, identities, knowledge, and strengths students bring with them. Through this work, the office supports students in developing academic confidence, belonging, purpose, and the ability to shape their educational journeys.

What we do

Support student transitions

Help students understand what to expect, connect with resources, and develop strategies for navigating college.

Build community and belonging

Create opportunities for students to connect with classmates, faculty, staff, campus programs, and the broader university community.

Strengthen first- and second-year learning

Support courses, learning communities, workshops, and experiences that build academic confidence and encourage purposeful exploration.

Connect students with resources

Help students learn about and access the many campus offices available to support their academic and personal success.

Partner across campus

Collaborate with faculty, staff, departments, and student support offices to create more coordinated first- and second-year experiences.

Support faculty and staff learning

Share research, student feedback, and campus data that can help faculty and staff better understand and support first- and second-year students.

Our approach

Students arrive at CSUDH with existing strengths, knowledge, responsibilities, relationships, and experiences. OFSYE works to recognize these strengths while helping students develop new academic strategies, relationships, and ways of navigating the university.

  • Equity and inclusion
  • Student strengths and lived experiences
  • Connection and belonging
  • Academic and personal growth
  • Cross-campus collaboration
  • Clear and accessible communication
  • Continuous learning from student experiences
